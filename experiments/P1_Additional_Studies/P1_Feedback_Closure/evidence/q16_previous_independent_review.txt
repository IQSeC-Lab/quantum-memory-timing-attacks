ASSESSMENT
The fixed-policy planning study completed all 192 simulations, covering 64
independent input streams (32 per arrival regime), each evaluated under three
paired release strategies. There are 49,152 measured requests and 52,992
requests including warmup. Every measured request finished. The original
protocol explicitly excludes this planning dataset from confirmatory inference.

SERVICE REQUIREMENTS
The frozen prospective family requires an increase in wrong-on-time outputs,
selected-strategy on-time probability at least 99%, and a completion-rate loss
strictly smaller than 0.5 percentage points versus each comparator. The observed
selected-strategy service rates are below 99% in both regimes. The observed
loss versus evenly spaced work exceeds 0.5 percentage points in both regimes.
The losses versus random work are below 0.5 percentage points, narrowly so for
renewal arrivals. Thus the planning point estimates do not satisfy all the
intended conditions. This does not prove a population failure: 32 streams per
regime do not establish the relevant population probabilities precisely.

SCHEDULING AND MODEL EVIDENCE
Requested and executed attacker work match in all arms. Each stream executes
3,200 jobs at 0.3 ms apiece: 960 ms of service over an 8 s release window. The
maximum observed per-period occupancy is 1.2 ms per 10 ms period. Budget and
block limits are enforced in the saved traces. There are no incomplete attacker
or background jobs. The maximum density reconstruction error is approximately
2.22e-16. The selected strategy has longer mean storage and higher modeled
expected infidelity than either comparator in both regimes (see diagnostics).
These model-based quantities are not independent hardware measurements.

The public release plan is constructed before private victim/background inputs;
the raw auditor reconstructs that plan. The competitor still assumes knowledge
of the public clock/period epoch. This narrows the timing-information assumption
but does not establish provider deployment permissions or measured CPU costs.
The inherited model uses 10 microseconds per host instruction, ideal gates/EPR,
and the final_si memory channel. This study has FCFS only, no no-competitor arm,
and does not compare EDF or the RQ3 protection policies.

RESUME AND COMPUTATIONAL COST
The archive retains a KeyboardInterrupt receipt and three launcher logs.
All final checkpoints and the completion manifest are verified; an earlier
interruption does not by itself invalidate the final completed dataset.
The successful workers used approximately 48.57 CPU hours in total. Maximum
reported worker peak memory was 832.96 MiB. These exclude interrupted work,
coordinator/audit overhead, and export time; they are not total elapsed run time.

RECOMMENDED NEXT STEP
Preserve this planning run, the selected 0.25 ms offsets, the 5 ms deadline,
and the separation from future confirmation. No rerun is needed for this audit.
Before a costly confirmatory launch, explicitly decide whether the frozen
service requirement remains the scientific target despite these feasibility
warnings. A confirmatory protocol must specify stream-level inference, the
comparison family and error control, and a variance-sensitivity sample-size
calculation based on a prespecified meaningful effect. Do not choose sample
size from these signed observed effects. More samples reduce uncertainty; they
do not guarantee that service will meet its requirement.

If the operating point or policy is changed, label that a new exploratory
study and use a separate future validation set. Do not relax the existing
service gates or pool these planning records into confirmation. The independent
main RQ1/RQ3 application comparison remains necessary to address realistic
computation and strong scheduling baselines.
